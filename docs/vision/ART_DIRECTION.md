## Creative thesis

HomeLab is a warm, inhabited retro-futuristic research campus embedded in nature. It should feel engineered and aspirational, never like a sterile technology showroom or a generic sci-fi corridor.

## Visual language

- Architecture: glass, black metal, subtle gold details, transparent roofs.
- Environment: forest, mountains, river or lake, plants, vertical gardens, fruit-bearing vegetation, water reflections.
- Day: natural light, sky, and clouds.
- Night: stars, planets, and restrained deep-sky presence.
- Avoid: massive concrete walls, neon overload, illegible holograms, logo walls without context, and ornamental effects that obscure content.

Nature must remain visible from major professional spaces. Technology belongs within the environment; it should not erase it.

## Composition and hierarchy

1. Establish destination and movement first.
2. Reveal one primary interaction per composition.
3. Keep professional evidence readable independently of decorative effects.
4. Use lighting and material contrast to separate traversable space, interactive systems, and ambience.
5. Reserve gold and saturated state colors for emphasis rather than general surface coverage.

## Zavit identity

Zavit is the friendly, intelligent robot butler of HomeLab: mostly black, white belly screen, expressive illuminated eyes whose color reflects state, head buttons, red claw hands, retro-futuristic and nostalgic character.

On arrival, Zavit is already doing something purposeful; never posed as a static receptionist. Exact proportions, animation language, eye-state palette, and activity are UNKNOWN until an accepted Zavit spec.

## Interaction presentation

- Guided Mode and Free Exploration must be visually distinguishable without blocking either route.
- Interactive targets need consistent focus, hover, active, unavailable, and error states.
- Technology appears through architecture components, workstations, technical panels, system labels, project context, and a Technology Wall—not logo soup.
- Simulation telemetry must visibly say `SIMULATION`.

## Accessible and responsive art direction

- Text and essential diagrams live in semantic UI, not textures alone.
- State is never communicated by color alone.
- Focus remains visible against bright glass, dark metal, day, and night contexts.
- Reduced motion replaces camera sweeps, parallax, and ambient loops with stable transitions.
- Adapted/mobile presentation may reduce geometry and effects while retaining identity and content priority.

## Future visual acceptance evidence
